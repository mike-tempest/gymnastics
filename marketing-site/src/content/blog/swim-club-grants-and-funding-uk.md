Most UK swimming clubs leave money on the table. Grant funding is available from Sport England, the National Lottery, Swim England regions and local councils, yet many committees never apply because the process looks daunting or they assume their club is too small to qualify. This guide to **grants and funding for swimming clubs** sets out exactly which schemes exist in 2026, what each one pays for, and how a busy volunteer committee can put together an application that actually succeeds.

The short answer: the largest realistic sources are the Sport England Movement Fund (£300 to £15,000), the National Lottery Community Fund's Awards for All England (£300 to £20,000), your regional Swim England club investment fund and bursaries, and your local council's community grants. Most of these fund equipment and participation projects rather than day-to-day running costs, so the trick is matching the right pot to the right need.

## Grants versus fundraising: which is which

Grants and fundraising solve different problems, and the strongest clubs use both. [Fundraising](/blog/swimming-club-fundraising-ideas-complete-guide/) (sponsored swims, quiz nights, raffles) is flexible income you can spend on anything, including running costs, but it relies on repeated volunteer effort. Grants are larger lump sums for specific, usually capital, projects, but they come with applications, deadlines and reporting.

A simple rule of thumb:

- **Capital costs** (starting blocks, pace clocks, a timing system, lane ropes, pool-access equipment, facility improvements): apply for a grant.
- **Running costs** (pool hire, coaching salaries, affiliation fees): cover with membership fees and regular fundraising.
- **One-off participation projects** (a new beginners' squad, a disability swimming session, an outreach programme): grants are well suited, because funders want to increase participation.

Knowing this distinction before you apply saves wasted effort. Asking the National Lottery to pay your pool hire will fail; asking it to fund a new learn-to-swim session for an under-served estate stands a real chance.

## The main grant funders for UK swimming clubs

### Sport England Movement Fund

The **Sport England Movement Fund** is the national grant programme for community sport in England, and it replaced the older Community Asset Fund. It offers grants from **£300 to £15,000** to not-for-profit organisations working with people in England. There is no fixed deadline; you should apply at least 12 weeks before you want to start, and decisions usually take six to eight weeks.

The fund prioritises getting more people active, reducing inactivity, and tackling long-standing inequalities, so frame your bid around participation and reaching people who currently miss out rather than around elite performance. Full criteria and the application portal are on the [Sport England Movement Fund page](https://www.sportengland.org/funding-and-campaigns/our-funding/movement-fund).

### National Lottery Awards for All England

**Awards for All England**, run by the National Lottery Community Fund, gives grants of **£300 to £20,000** for up to two years (the ceiling doubled from £10,000 in November 2023). It funds community-led projects that bring people together, improve local spaces, or help people reach their potential, which a swimming club's outreach or junior development work fits neatly.

It is one of the most accessible funders for grassroots clubs because the application is relatively short and there is no requirement to be a registered charity. See the [Awards for All England programme page](https://www.tnlcommunityfund.org.uk/funding/funding-programmes/national-lottery-awards-for-all-england) for current guidance.

### Swim England regional and county funding

Swim England distributes funding through its **regions and county associations** rather than as a single national pot, so the schemes vary by where your club is based. Across the regions you will find club investment or development funds for equipment and projects, plus **bursaries** that part-fund coaching and officiating qualifications for individual volunteers. Amounts, deadlines and names differ by region, so the only reliable approach is to contact your Swim England regional team or county development officer and ask what is currently open.

Swim England's [club finances hub](https://www.swimming.org/swimengland/club-finances/) is the starting point, and from time to time wider hardship or recovery funds open in partnership with Sport England, so it is worth checking periodically.

### Local council community grants

Most district, borough and unitary councils run **community grant schemes**, typically between £250 and £5,000, for local groups that benefit residents. These are often overlooked, the competition is more local, and councils like to support visible community assets such as a swimming club. Search your council's website for "community grants" or "small grants" and read the eligibility notes before applying.

### Active Partnerships and Sported

Your local **Active Partnership** (the network formerly known as County Sports Partnerships, such as Active Surrey or Active Essex) does not usually hand out large grants directly, but it knows every local funding stream and can point your club to the right one and help with applications. They are a free, under-used resource.

**Sported** is a charity that offers free membership and funding support to community sports groups. Note that its own Cash4Clubs grant scheme is aimed at clubs supporting **adults** (18 and over), so it will not suit a junior-focused swimming club, but Sported's wider guidance and partner funding can still be useful.

## A note on the rest of the UK

This guide is England-focused, but the structure is similar across the UK. In Scotland, funding flows through **sportscotland** and **Scottish Swimming**; in Wales through **Sport Wales** and **Swim Wales**; and in Northern Ireland through **Sport Northern Ireland** and **Swim Ireland**. The National Lottery Community Fund also runs equivalent Awards for All programmes in each nation, so clubs outside England should look to their own national governing body and national lottery portal.

## CASC registration: the funding multiplier

Registering as a **Community Amateur Sports Club (CASC)** with HMRC is one of the most valuable financial steps a club can take, and it strengthens almost every grant application.

The benefits are significant:

- **Gift Aid** adds 25 per cent to qualifying donations from UK taxpayers (£25 for every £100 given).
- **80 per cent mandatory business rates relief** on premises the club uses, with local authorities able to grant the remaining 20 per cent at their discretion.
- **Corporation tax exemptions** on most trading and property income within set thresholds.

CASC status also signals good governance to funders, who want evidence that public money will be well looked after. Check eligibility and register at [gov.uk/community-amateur-sports-clubs](https://www.gov.uk/community-amateur-sports-clubs). Note that CASC and charity status are mutually exclusive, so take advice before choosing between them.

## How to write a grant application that wins

Grant assessors read hundreds of bids. The ones that succeed are specific, evidenced and easy to say yes to.

1. **Be precise about what the money buys.** "£4,200 for eight new starting blocks to replace blocks beyond their safe service life" beats "money for equipment" every time. Attach a quote.
2. **Evidence the need.** Use real figures: membership numbers, a waiting list, the age of the kit you are replacing, the catchment you serve. Clean membership records make this painless. Read our [treasurer's guide to club finances](/blog/managing-swim-club-finances/) for how to keep the numbers funders ask for.
3. **Lead with participation and inclusion.** Funders want more people active, especially under-represented groups. If a grant lets you open a session for disabled swimmers, a deprived estate or an under-served age group, say so plainly.
4. **Show good governance.** Funders look for a constitution, a properly run club bank account, recent accounts and current safeguarding. Your [Swim England affiliation and Wavepower compliance](/blog/what-wavepower-compliance-actually-requires/) are strong evidence here.
5. **Have your paperwork ready.** Most applications want a governing document, last year's accounts, a bank statement and a safeguarding policy. Keep a "funding folder" so you are never scrambling to a deadline.
6. **Plan the spend and the reporting.** Show a simple budget and say how you will report back on what the money achieved. Funders reward clubs that close the loop.

Appointing one committee member to lead on funding, and presenting a clear funding target at your [AGM](/blog/running-your-first-swimming-club-agm/), turns grants from a scramble into a routine. Even one successful application a year can transform what a club can offer.

## Build grants into your annual plan

Grants work best as one strand of a deliberate financial plan rather than an emergency measure. Pair them with a realistic [budget](/blog/swim-club-budget-planning-guide/) and a steady [fundraising calendar](/blog/swimming-club-fundraising-ideas-complete-guide/), and the club gains the stability to plan ahead instead of lurching from one cash crisis to the next. A typical rhythm might be: one major grant application each year for a capital project, two or three fundraising events, and CASC-backed Gift Aid quietly topping up every donation.

The clubs that fund themselves well are not the ones chasing every pot. They pick the one or two funders that genuinely match a real need, prepare properly, and apply once a year with a strong, well-evidenced bid.

## Frequently asked questions

### What grants can a UK swimming club apply for?

The main grant sources for UK swimming clubs are the Sport England Movement Fund (£300 to £15,000), the National Lottery Community Fund's Awards for All England (£300 to £20,000), regional and county Swim England club investment funds and bursaries, and local council community grants. Most fund equipment and projects that increase participation rather than day-to-day running costs.

### Can a swimming club get funding for running costs like pool hire?

Rarely from grants. Most funders cover capital costs (equipment, facility improvements) or specific participation projects, not core running costs such as pool hire and coaching salaries. Those are best covered by membership fees and regular fundraising. The exception is short-term hardship or recovery funds that open periodically, so check your Swim England region for current schemes.

### Does my swimming club need to be a charity to apply for grants?

No. Most clubs apply as constituted community groups or Community Amateur Sports Clubs (CASCs). Funders generally require a formal governing document (a constitution), a club bank account with at least two unrelated signatories, and basic accounts. Registering as a CASC with HMRC is not essential to apply but strengthens applications and unlocks Gift Aid and 80 per cent business rates relief.

### How do I write a strong grant application for a swimming club?

Be specific about what the money buys and who benefits, evidence the need with membership and waiting-list data, show good governance (constitution, accounts, safeguarding and Wavepower compliance), and explain the wider community impact beyond competitive swimming. Funders favour projects that increase participation among under-represented groups, so make any inclusion angle clear.

### What is the difference between a grant and a bursary for swim clubs?

A grant is usually awarded to the club for equipment or a project. A bursary is typically awarded to an individual, most often to part-fund a coaching or official qualification. Swim England regions run both, so a club can apply for a project grant while its volunteers separately apply for course bursaries.
